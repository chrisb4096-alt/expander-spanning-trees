\section{Open questions}\label{sec:open}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Large girth (answered).} An earlier version of this paper asked whether the uniform spanning tree of a bounded-degree expander of girth $\Omega(\log N)$, such as the Ramanujan graphs of Lubotzky, Phillips and Sarnak~\cite{LPS88}, needs $2^{\Omega(N)}$-size nonnegative decomposable PCs at constant total-variation error. Theorem~\ref{thm:main} answers this for every such graph that is 2-connected, which includes the Ramanujan graphs of~\cite{LPS88} and random $d$-regular graphs with probability tending to one (Corollary~\ref{cor:families}). For bounded-degree expanders that are not 2-connected, Theorem~\ref{thm:blocks} describes what balanced splits can give in terms of the largest block. It remains open whether 2-connected bounded-degree graphs with much weaker expansion, such as $h(G)\to0$ slowly, still need superpolynomial size; the grid (Corollary~\ref{cor:grid}) is one such case.

\item \emph{Unbounded degree (partly answered).} An earlier version asked for a constant-error exponential lower bound for $\UST(K_n)$. Corollary~\ref{cor:complete} gives one, of the optimal order $2^{\Theta(n)}$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:complete-order}), and Corollary~\ref{cor:dense} gives $2^{\Omega(n)}$ for every graph with $\lambda_2(L_G)\ge\kappa n$, with a rate of order $\kappa^4e^{-1.14/\kappa}$. A minimum degree of $n/2$ alone does not make balanced splits weakly correlated (Proposition~\ref{prop:two-cliques}). Between bounded degree and a linear spectral gap we have no good bound. In particular, for expanders whose degree grows with $N$, and for products or powers of expanders, $\ln(1/c(D,h))$ grows like $D\log D$ even for fixed $h$, the spectral constant $c_\lambda(D)$ decays like $D^{-4}$, the cycle weights are $D^{-\ell}$, and Theorem~\ref{thm:dense} conditions on fewer than $\lambda_2$ vertices. How does the exponent depend on the degree? And does $\UST$ of the two cliques of Proposition~\ref{prop:two-cliques} need circuits of size $2^{\Omega(n)}$ at every fixed error?

\item \emph{Constants.} The constant in Example~\ref{ex:cubic} is about $3\cdot10^{-7}$, $c(D,h)$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is $5.2\cdot10^{-41}$ in closed form and about $5\cdot10^{-24}$ with optimized parameters for $D=3$ and $h=1/5$ (Table~\ref{tab:constants}), and $8.0\cdot10^{-8}$ for 6-regular Ramanujan graphs. For $K_n$, Theorem~\ref{thm:complete} gives $-\ln\beta^2\ge n/146-O(\log n)$, while exact computations for $n\le9$ give $-\ln\beta^2$ between $0.12n$ and $0.30n$ on the splits we tried, and a Monte Carlo upper estimate of the true rate is about $0.17n$ (Remark~\ref{rem:kn-ceiling}). Evaluated directly for particular splits, Theorem~\ref{thm:touch-cut} gives exponents of order $10^{-3}N$ on random cubic graphs (Appendix~\ref{app:checks}), so most of the loss is in the worst-case steps. What is the correct exponent for, say, cubic Ramanujan graphs, their prisms, the $L\times L$ grid, or $K_n$? Natural improvements are a sharper local lemma, using all mixed cycles or all mixed vertices rather than a sparse subfamily, typical rather than worst-case resistances, and a direct bound on $\opnorm{\sqrt\mu}$ for the joint law of all the bits. Is $c(D,h)\ge\operatorname{poly}(h)$ with a degree independent of $D$, or at least $c\ge1/\operatorname{poly}(D,1/h)$? Star bits cannot show either: on the hub graphs of Section~\ref{sec:star-limits}, which are 2-connected with maximum degree $D$, they certify at most $O(h^{D/2+1})$ for one balanced split (Corollary~\ref{cor:hub}). Edge pairs give $h^3/(10935D^5)$ for that split (Proposition~\ref{prop:hub-pairs}), and Conjecture~\ref{conj:pairs} below would give a polynomial constant for every split.

\item \emph{Other models.} Does the bound extend to nonnegative circuits that are decomposable but not smooth, with a size measure independent of smoothing? For structured circuits Corollary~\ref{cor:signed-expander} already does this, with a smaller exponent. To structured or deterministic variants with sharper constants? To positive semidefinite circuit models not covered by Theorem~\ref{thm:signed-square}~\cite{LoconteMengelVergari25,WangVdB25}?

\item \emph{Unstructured subtraction (partly answered).} Must a smooth decomposable circuit with real weights and univariate leaves, with no common vtree, that computes $Q$ with $\frac12\norm{\UST(G)-Q}_1\le\eps<\frac12$ on a bounded-degree 2-connected expander have size $e^{\Omega(N)}$? Even superpolynomial? With a common vtree, Theorem~\ref{thm:signed-o2} and Corollary~\ref{cor:signed-expander} answer this for every error below $1$ when $Q$ sums to $1$. Without one, Corollary~\ref{cor:signed-unstructured} answers the support-exact question for bounded product-depth and for formulas, on expanders with large complete minors, and Theorem~\ref{thm:signed-sums-linear} answers the support-exact and the approximate question for sums of a bounded number of structured circuits, on every 2-connected bounded-degree expander. This leaves general circuits, where exact representation is as hard as lower bounds for syntactically multilinear circuits computing the spanning-tree polynomial (Proposition~\ref{prop:signed-multilinear}), approximate versions of Theorems~\ref{thm:signed-depth} and~\ref{thm:signed-formula}, and planar families such as the grid. Section~\ref{sec:signed-barriers} shows why norm-type measures and fixed cycle blocks cannot give the approximate versions. Is there a support-exact lower bound for the grid? Can the constants of Theorem~\ref{thm:signed-sums-linear} be made reasonable? By Theorem~\ref{thm:signed-cancel}, a small approximating circuit would have to cancel all but an exponentially small fraction of its absolute mass.

\item \emph{Other determinantal distributions.} The local proof uses only a forbidden local pattern and a spatial Markov property, and the vertex-touch proof adds Kirchhoff's formula for one edge. Section~\ref{sec:matroid} carries both over to uniform bases of matroids. Which other distributions with determinant-based inference, such as rooted spanning forests, whose weighted count is $\det(L+qI)$, admit the same argument on expander-like families?

\item \emph{Matroids.} Does Theorem~\ref{thm:mat-global}, or some global version of it, hold for every matroid? Lemma~\ref{lem:mat-global} fails for $S_8$ (Section~\ref{sec:limits}), but we do not know whether the theorem fails. For regular matroids, is there an analogue of Lemmas~\ref{lem:touch-flow} and~\ref{lem:delete}: a condition playing the role of expansion that keeps the resistances $R_Z(e)$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:mat-resist} bounded after a sparse set is deleted? Which natural matroid families, beyond graphs, their duals and the Fano-decorated family, satisfy the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:mat-pc}?
\end{enumerate}

The edge-pair chain of Theorem~\ref{thm:edge-pair} suggests one route to polynomial constants. For a vertex $w$ with an edge $e=uw\in A$ and an edge $f=vw\in B$, and a pattern $\sigma$ on earlier pairs, let $Y_\sigma(e,f)$ be the quantity of Lemma~\ref{lem:edge-pair-law} in the graph $K_\sigma$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:edge-pair}.

\begin{conjecture}\label{conj:pairs}
There is a polynomial $P$ such that for every 2-connected graph $G$ with maximum degree $D$ and $h(G)\ge h>0$, and every balanced split $A|B$, there are vertices $w_1,\dots,w_r$ with $r\ge N/P(D,1/h)$ and edges $e_k\in A$ and $f_k\in B$ at $w_k$, all $2r$ edges distinct, such that, for every $k$ and every pattern $\sigma$ on the earlier pairs with positive probability, neither $e_k$ nor $f_k$ is a loop of $K_\sigma$ and $Y_\sigma(e_k,f_k)\ge1/P(D,1/h)$.
\end{conjecture}

By Theorem~\ref{thm:edge-pair} and Lemma~\ref{lem:edge-pair-law}, Conjecture~\ref{conj:pairs} would give $c\ge1/\operatorname{poly}(D,1/h)$ for all 2-connected bounded-degree expanders. The 2-connectivity is needed. Attach a triangle at each vertex of a cubic expander $H$ on $n$ vertices, with two new vertices per triangle. The result has $N=3n$, $M=9n/2$, maximum degree $5$ and $h(G)\ge\min(h(H)/3,1)$, and the split $A=E(H)$, $B=$ the triangle edges is balanced. The uniform spanning tree is an independent product of a spanning tree of $H$ and one spanning tree of each triangle, so $\beta(\UST(G);A|B)=1$ and every $Y$ between an edge of $A$ and an edge of $B$ vanishes. For random regular graphs with $D\in\{3,4\}$ and $N\le1000$, sampled values of $Y_\sigma$ on spread-out mixed vertices stayed within a factor $0.81$ of the tree-like value $(D-2)/(D(D-1))$; this is numerical evidence for fixed graphs only, and we have no proof.
